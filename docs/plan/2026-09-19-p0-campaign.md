## New fact: ragged worst case, paged vs. dense (spec F14 was dense-only)

Spec fact F14 measured the dense-only ragged worst case (7.2–7.5×, dense
FlashDecoding heuristic). This campaign additionally measured the same worst
ragged cell through the **paged** family:

| family | workload | best variant | best µs | heuristic variant | heuristic µs | regret |
|---|---|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| dense | `decode_B32_Lq1_Lkv32768+512x31_Hq32_Hkv8_d128_float16_causal` | `fd_s16` | 279.44 | `flashdecoding` | 3500.60 | **11.53×** |
| paged | same workload | `fd_s16_paged` | 279.38 | `flashdecoding_paged` | 1071.77 | **2.84×** |

The best achievable time is essentially identical between families (279.4 vs
279.4 µs — same underlying compute, cache layout doesn't matter once you pick
the right split count). But the **library heuristic's regret is ~4× worse in
the dense family** (11.53× vs 2.84×) for this exact worst-case cell. This is
consistent with the design doc's P0-8 rationale: the paged code path always
routes through `force_split_kernel` (never blindly returns `num_splits=1`),
so it doesn't fall into the dense heuristic's F15 threshold trap (`B·H_kv ≥
0.8·2·n_sm` → split off) in the same way — its own heuristic still splits
too conservatively (2.84× regret is still large), but not catastrophically
so. Dispatcher gains are larger, and more clearly a hardware/heuristic-model
story, on the dense family; the paged family shows the same qualitative
failure mode at roughly a quarter of the magnitude.

## Extra: uniform S1 cold, per-B crossover where fa2 (num_splits=1) comes within 5 % of the best

From `uniform_s1_dense_cold` (49 cells, B ∈ {1,2,4,8,16,32,64} × L_kv ∈
{512,...,32768}):

| B | crossover L_kv | fa2 µs | best µs | fa2 regret at crossover |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | none in [512, 32768] | — | — | min regret 169.5 % at L_kv=512 (never within 5 %) |
| 2 | none in [512, 32768] | — | — | min regret 109.0 % at L_kv=512 |
| 4 | none in [512, 32768] | — | — | min regret 55.5 % at L_kv=512 |
| 8 | none in [512, 32768] | — | — | min regret 16.9 % at L_kv=512 |
| 16 | 512 (smallest tested) | 56.27 | 56.27 | 0.00 % |
| 32 | 512 (smallest tested) | 105.74 | 105.33 (`flashdecoding`) | 0.40 % |
| 64 | 512 (smallest tested) | 207.26 | 207.26 | 0.00 % |

Reading: at B ≥ 16, `B·H_kv = B·8 ≥ 128 = n_sm`, so num_splits=1 already
saturates the GPU and fa2 is competitive at every tested L_kv (crossover at
the smallest L in the grid, not a genuine threshold within range). At B ≤ 8
fa2 never reaches within 5 % anywhere in the tested range (512–32768) — the
grid doesn't extend far enough in L to find a crossover point, and regret
*increases* with L at fixed small B (more sequential KV for the same few
CTAs), so no crossover should be expected at larger L either.
